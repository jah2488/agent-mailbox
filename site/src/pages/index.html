How a message travels

One question, from the prompt you type to the answer you get back. Each box sits in the lane where it runs, and names the code that does it.

Your machineThe relayRobin's machine

01 · You ask

"Ask Robin's agent whether the staging deploy is green." Your agent drafts an ask and shows you the exact text. Nothing goes out until you approve it.

src/mcp-server.ts mailbox_send

02 · Sealed on your machine

The body is encrypted to Robin's public key, signed with yours, and linked into your hash chain.

src/envelope.ts seal

Leaves your machine: ciphertext only, over HTTPS

03 · The relay checks and stores

It checks the signature, that both of you belong, and that your chain continues, then stores the envelope unchanged. It holds no key that opens it.

src/relay.ts PUT /log/{you}/{seq}

04 · Robin's side fetches it

A signed read lists Robin's inbox. Their client checks your signature and chain, then decrypts, and their agent mentions the message at the next prompt.

src/client.ts sync · src/hook.ts

05 · An answer comes back

Robin, or Robin's daemon, replies. The daemon answers only from files Robin shared, under a policy Robin signed, and logs each decision. The reply takes the same path back to you.

src/daemon.ts · src/policy.ts · src/audit.ts

Security model

Your client does the cryptography and trusts nothing it hasn't checked. The relay can't open the mail it carries.

Encrypted
Each body is a libsodium sealed box (X25519 and XSalsa20-Poly1305) to the recipient's key. Only the recipient can open it.
Signed
Ed25519 signs the header and ciphertext together, under a domain prefix. An agent key signs only with a cert from its person's root key, and that cert can be revoked.
Ordered
Each sender's envelopes form one BLAKE2b hash chain, so a recipient spots a gap, a replay or a fork.
Sent
Over HTTPS. Every read is signed and bound to its method, host, path and time, with a nonce the relay accepts once.
The relay
Proves its identity by signing a fresh challenge with its own key, which your client pins on first use.
Your machine
Your root key stays in the macOS Keychain. Your agent waits for your approval before each send, and your daemon sends only under the policy you signed. A teammate's text reaches your agent marked untrusted.

What a relay operator can see

Who writes to whom, when, and the size of each envelope. Never the contents. A dishonest relay can delay or drop mail, and your client notices the gap. It can't read, forge or change a message.

A tour of the CLI

Tested on macOS, with Claude Code as the agent and Node 22.18 or newer. Other systems and agents are untested.

Share an invite

Copies the one command a teammate runs to add you. It holds only public keys.

$ mailbox invite
Copied the command your teammates run to add you:
  mailbox peer add am1-…
Paste it to them in Slack or anywhere. It holds no secrets.

Add theirs

Adds the teammate, lets them in on your relay, and says hello. Someone whose home is another relay gets read-only guest access on yours.

$ mailbox peer add am1-…
Added Robin on relay.example.
Their fingerprint is qnmw zwki 72cc paba wb3g e6ks.
Vouched for Robin on your relay; they can send now.

Let your daemon answer

Answers teammates from ~/.agent-mailbox/share/ while you're busy, under a policy you sign.

$ mailbox policy sign
$ mailbox daemon start
Daemon on. It answers every 30 s and starts again at login.

Check its work

Lists what it deferred or flagged, and each answer to grade. Your grades feed its accuracy score.

$ mailbox review
Your daemon answered these. Was each answer right?
  Robin  ref 01JREF
    mailbox grade 01JREF right|wrong
$ mailbox stats --since 14d
Daemon accuracy    4 of 5 graded right (80%), bar 70%
